# Finish the basics, then own the browser

Across all six categories, the features people use most are small, fast and repeated every day. They capture a task in a sentence, fire a reminder, repeat a task, block a distracting site on a schedule, find something they saved, and have AI write, search or summarise for them. The big, heavily marketed features (autonomous agents, AI auto-scheduling, whiteboards, team workspaces) have the weakest usage evidence and the most churn. ProdLi already covers more ground than almost any single competitor: an AI daily brief, Plan your day and Wrap up, chat over the user's own data, no-bot Meet notes, Pomodoro, habits and per-site limits. Its gaps sit inside the basics, not in missing categories. The to-do layer has no recurring tasks, no per-task reminders, and no lists, labels or saved filters. The focus layer has limits but no block schedules or blocking tied to focus sessions. Capture has right-click saving but no tab sessions or read-later list. Meetings has no templates per meeting type, no citations in chat, and no coverage outside Google Meet. The next store update should close those gaps with features that need **no new permissions**: recurring tasks and reminders, lists and labels, full natural-language quick add, scheduled site blocking with a pause screen, saved tab sessions, new-tab quick links and search, and meeting templates. After that, extend no-bot capture to Zoom and Teams on the web and add voice capture. Everything Chrome now does natively (Gemini page summaries, reading mode, split view, tab groups) is off the list, and so are auto-scheduling and agents. One caveat runs through everything below: hard per-feature usage data barely exists. Most of the evidence is Chrome Web Store install counts, a few company telemetry disclosures, workplace AI surveys and one field study. Reviewer opinion and vendor marketing fill in the rest.

## Six categories converge on capture, reminders, search and summaries

The evidence is uneven, so it helps to name its grades first. True usage data comes from a few places: Chrome Web Store (CWS) weekly active installs (rounded to the leading digit), Todoist's telemetry on its Ramble voice feature, Notion's share of customers paying for AI, Zoom's earnings commentary, OpenAI's study of 1.5 million conversations, Gallup and Census workplace surveys, and two peer-reviewed studies of One Sec and Freedom. Everything else is reviewer judgment (Zapier, G2, Capterra), vendor blogs that often compare themselves to rivals, or feature pages. **No vendor publishes what share of its users use a given feature.** "Most used" below therefore means features with measured adoption where that exists, and otherwise features that appear in every leader, sit on the free tier, and draw praise in reviews.

| Category | Most-used / most relied-on features | Strongest evidence | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workspaces & notes | Rich notes with checklists, search, templates, database/table views, web clipping, AI meeting notes, Q&A over notes, AI rewrite | Clipper installs; Obsidian plugin downloads; Notion AI payer share | Usage proxies + company claims |
| Tasks & planners | Natural-language quick add, due dates and reminders, recurring tasks, lists/labels/filters, Today/Inbox, sync, calendar visibility; AI voice capture | Todoist Ramble telemetry; CWS installs | Company telemetry + reviews |
| Calendars & time-blocking | Daily planning ritual, drag-to-timebox, estimates, shutdown review; AI auto-scheduling (heavily marketed) | Reviewer comparisons; G2 ratings; shutdowns | Reviews + market events |
| Focus, habits, time tracking | Block lists, schedules, daily limits, focus-session blocking, lock mode, pause screens; habit streaks, count/timed/quit habits, heatmaps; passive tracking with a focus score | CWS installs; One Sec and Freedom studies | Usage + peer-reviewed research |
| Chrome extensions & new tabs | AI writing help, translation, screenshots, tab saving, clipping, site blocking; on new tabs: clock, focus, to-dos, links, search, backgrounds | CWS top-500 ranking (17 Nov 2026) | Usage (installs) |
| AI & meeting assistants | Search/research, drafting/rewriting, summarising; meetings: summary + action items, chat across meetings, templates, recap sharing, no-bot capture | OpenAI, Gallup, Pythian surveys; Otter/Zoom disclosures | Usage surveys + company claims |

### Workspaces reward capture and structure, not breadth

Notion dominates the category with **100M+ users and 4M+ paying customers** ([AI Magazine](https://aimagazine.com/news/notion-ai-agents-for-a-workspace-used-by-100-million-people); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/notion/)). It also holds the only hard AI-adoption numbers: the share of customers paying for AI rose from 10–20% to **over 50%** in about a year ([newsio/CNBC](https://newsio.com/2025/09/18/notion-rides-ai-boom-to-500-million-in-annual-revenue-but-microsoft-competition-looms/?amp=1)), and users built over 1 million Custom Agents in two months ([AI Magazine](https://aimagazine.com/news/notion-ai-agents-for-a-workspace-used-by-100-million-people)). "Agents built" is not "agents used", though. The clearest behavioural signal is web clipping. Evernote's clipper has **about 2M users at 4.5 stars** ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/evernote-web-clipper/pioclpoplcdbaefihamjohnefbikjilc)), OneNote's 1M at 4.8 ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/onenote-web-clipper/gojbdfnpnhogfdgjbigejoaolejmgdhk?hl=en)), and Notion's 1M at just 2.9 ([chromeextensions.org](https://www.chromeextensions.org/top/4/)). Obsidian publishes no user count, but its plugin downloads show what power users add. Excalidraw has about 7.4–9.1M downloads, while Dataview (database queries) and Templater (templates) have about 5.3M each ([ObsidianStats](https://www.obsidianstats.com/most-downloaded)). Templates and structured views are what people go out of their way to install.

The complaints matter as much as the features. One vendor analysis found **sync failures and data loss behind 22% of low-star note-app reviews** ([unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/notion-evernote-obsidian-apple-notes-onenote-note-taking-apps-ranked-2026)). It is a single source with an unclear method, but it matches the theme. Notion is criticised for slow quick capture, especially on mobile ([Fibery](https://fibery.io/openion/notion-2)), and for search that misses content ([2sync](https://2sync.com/blog/is-notion-a-good-note-taking-app)). Bending Spoons says usage limits drove **51% of Evernote's 2025 subscription conversions** ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/the-pulse-bending-spoons-acquisition-strategy/)). The same pricing produced a loud backlash, which makes limits a powerful but dangerous lever.

### Task apps win on speed of capture and reminders, not bundled extras

Todoist claims **more than 50 million users** ([AI Journal](https://aijourn.com/?p=473459)), and its Chrome extension has 500K users ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/todoist-for-chrome-planne/jldhpllghnbhlbpcmnajkpdmadaolakh)). Its Gmail extension adds another 100K ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/todoist-for-gmail-planner/clgenfnodoocmhnlnpknojdbjjnmecff)). Zapier ranks it #1 for natural-language entry, an Inbox, projects, labels and filters. Zapier's stated must-haves are fast adding and organising, multiple ways to organise, reminders for self-imposed deadlines, a clean interface, and sync everywhere ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/best-to-do-list-apps/)). The best-measured AI feature anywhere in the to-do category is Todoist Ramble, which turns speech into structured tasks. **About 76,000 testers ran about 290,000 sessions in three weeks**, task-creation success rose from about 40% to 62%, and Doist says new entry-level users upgraded at **about 5x the previous rate** after using it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/todoists-app-now-lets-you-add-tasks-to-your-to-do-list-by-speaking-to-its-ai/)). TickTick bundles Pomodoro, habits, an Eisenhower matrix and a calendar, yet its Chrome extension has only 100K users ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ticktick-todo-task-list/diankknpkndanachmlckaikddgcehkod)). Feature breadth does not predict popularity. Zapier explicitly gave no credit for AI scheduling or subtask suggestions ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/best-to-do-list-apps/)). The most common complaints are paywalled basics: Todoist's filters and labels, and TickTick's calendar view ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/ticktick/about)).

### Time-blocking is a ritual, and AI auto-scheduling is a churning niche

Sunsama and Akiflow define the daily-planning loop: pick today's tasks, estimate them, drag them onto the calendar, and end with a shutdown review ([Ellie Planner](https://ellieplanner.com/comparisons/sunsama-vs-akiflow)). They charge roughly $20–34 a month for it ([Saner.ai](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/sunsama-vs-akiflow)). AI auto-scheduling is the opposite story. Clockwise **shut down on 27 March 2026 and deleted data for 40,000 organisations** ([Doodle](https://doodle.com/en/clockwise-is-shutting-down-what-to-do-next-in-2026/)). Dropbox absorbed Reclaim ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/22/dropbox-acquires-index-ventures-backed-ai-scheduling-tool-reclaim-ai/?rand=23331)). Motion moved upmarket into "AI Employees" ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/motion)). Motion rates 4.1 on G2, with complaints about its learning curve and rigidity ([G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/motionapp-vs-reclaim-ai-vs-calendly)), and it offers no suggest-then-approve mode ([Morgen](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-review)). Meanwhile Notion bundles calendar scheduling at no extra cost ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/motion)).

### Focus tools that work add friction on a schedule

The largest browser blockers are BlockSite (about **1M users, 4.5 stars**) and StayFocusd (about 700K, 4.4). BlockSite keeps scheduling off its free plan. StayFocusd gives away its uncancellable "Nuclear Option" ([SiteKeeper](https://www.sitekeeper.tech/compare/stayfocusd-vs-blocksite)). The best evidence in the whole focus category comes from One Sec's pause screen. In a PNAS field study, **36% of attempted app opens were abandoned and actual opens fell 57% after six weeks**. A follow-up experiment found that an explicit "close" option drove the effect, and a reflective message alone did not ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409)). That study is from 2023, and the founder is a co-author. The Freedom randomised trial adds a warning: only **25.5% of committed participants complied** with a two-week block ([PNAS Nexus](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017)), so setup friction kills adoption. Top habit apps converge on count, timed and quit habits, skip or vacation days that keep streaks, and heatmaps ([HabitKit guide](https://habitkit.app/help/guides/best-habit-tracker-apps), a competitor's guide). Automatic trackers such as Rize sell a daily focus score built from passively collected data ([dhruvirzala](https://dhruvirzala.com/rize-review/)).

### In Chrome, AI writing dwarfs dashboards, and trust decides ratings

The CWS top-500 ranking shows where browser usage actually goes. Grammarly has **40M users**, Claude 23M, ChatGPT 9M, Sider 5M, GoFullPage (screenshots) 11M, and OneTab and Momentum about 2M each ([chromeextensions.org](https://www.chromeextensions.org/top/)). The Claude figure was not independently verified. New-tab dashboards are a niche with a ceiling of about 2M (Momentum). The best-loved one, Bonjourr (**about 300K users, 4.9 stars**), wins by being free, private and account-free ([Dashy](https://www.dashyapp.com/blog/best-new-tab-extensions-2026)). Its core is a clock, background, links and a search box. Momentum's most-criticised theme is paywall placement, at 20 of 44 mentions negative ([Tooltivity](https://tooltivity.com/extensions/momentum)). Tab savers are widely installed but poorly rated: OneTab has 3.8 stars and Session Buddy 3.3 ([chromeextensions.org](https://www.chromeextensions.org/top/3/)). In February 2026 a storage migration emptied many OneTab users' lists ([Tooltivity](https://tooltivity.com/extensions/onetab)). Tactiq, the closest Chrome-native analogue to ProdLi's Meet notes, has **1M users at 4.9 stars** ([chromeextensions.org](https://www.chromeextensions.org/top/3/)).

### People use AI to write, find and summarise, not to act for them

OpenAI's study of 1.5 million conversations found that **writing is the most common work task** and that 49% of messages ask for information or advice ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/)). In Pythian's survey, research (66%), drafting (64%), summarising (43%) and finding documents across sources (42%) top the list ([Pythian](https://www.pythian.com/blog/ai-in-the-workplace-employee-productivity-sentiment-survey)). Daily AI use at work is still a minority habit: Gallup found 10% in Q3 2025, and daily-plus-weekly use reached 28% in Q1 2026 ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/half-of-all-us-employees-now-use-artificial-intelligence-at-work-crossing-landmark-threshold-for-first-time-gallup-data-shows-daily-and-weekly-usage-hitting-all-time-high-of-28-percent-in-q1-2026-with-65-percent-feeling-positive-about-its-impact-on-productivity)). Proactive briefings are consolidating. OpenAI sunset ChatGPT Pulse in June 2026 and folded it into "Scheduled tasks" ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260619-chatgpt-scheduled-tasks/)). Among meeting assistants, Otter reports **35M+ users and $100M ARR** ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251222704206/en/Otter.ai-Caps-Transformational-2025-with-100M-ARR-Milestone-Industry-first-AI-Meeting-Agents-and-Global-Enterprise-Expansion/)). Zoom AI Companion's monthly actives grew about 4x year over year, and usage is spreading into meeting prep and post-meeting tasks ([Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/zoom-delivers-strong-q2-sees-ai-usage-surge)). Granola, which records without a bot, reached a reported **$1.5B valuation** in March 2026 ([TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/granola-series-c-meeting-ai-enterprise-context)). Notion copied its system-audio approach ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/product/ai-meeting-notes)). The loudest meeting complaint is bots: "every meeting we have I have to kick several of these AI notetakers," one host wrote ([Zoom Community](https://community.zoom.com/t5/Zoom-Meetings/How-do-I-disable-AI-Notetakers-otter-ai-read-ai-fireflies-ai-etc/td-p/233848)). Every leader now ships the same set: summary with action items, chat across meetings with citations, templates per meeting type, and automatic recap sharing ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-meeting-assistant/); [Granola help](https://help.granola.ai/article/chatting-with-your-meetings)).

## ProdLi has the breadth; its gaps sit inside the basics

Measured against these norms, ProdLi is unusually complete for a Chrome extension. The v1.3.0 source also includes a **Plan your day flow (pick tasks, estimate, check fit) and a Wrap up step (move the rest to tomorrow, short reflection)**. Together with the daily brief and time blocks, that already gives ProdLi the Sunsama ritual that costs $20+ a month elsewhere, and the store listing should say so. Chat over the user's own notes, tasks and meetings matches the converging "Q&A over my stuff" feature that Notion, Evernote, OneNote and NotebookLM all shipped. No-bot Meet capture sits on the category's growth vector. The gaps are concentrated in the daily-repeat mechanics that keep users coming back.

The differentiators worth pursuing follow from ProdLi's existing strengths. **No-bot capture** answers the category's top complaint, but it currently covers only Google Meet, while Granola and Notion record device audio and work everywhere. **Privacy** is a stronger asset than it looks. ShadyPanda, a campaign that hid in extensions including a new-tab extension, affected **4.3M Chrome and Edge users** through silent malicious updates ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/12/01/chrome_edge_malicious_browser_extensions/)). PayPal Honey lost about 3M of its 20M users within two weeks of a trust scandal ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal_Honey)). Bonjourr's 4.9 rating rests on collecting nothing. The **new-tab hub** speaks directly to tool sprawl: workers switch tabs or apps **33 times a day** ([Lokalise](https://lokalise.com/blog/blog-tool-fatigue-productivity-report/)) and are interrupted every two minutes ([Microsoft WorkLab](https://microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday)), so "already connected and searchable" is the pitch, not feature count. **AI with its own allowances** fits where the market is going: Notion moved agents to purchasable credits ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/releases/2026-02-24)), and Todoist limits Ramble sessions on free plans ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/todoists-app-now-lets-you-add-tasks-to-your-to-do-list-by-speaking-to-its-ai/)). ProdLi's AI should work on the user's own saved data, where Gemini in Chrome does not reach.

## A three-tier roadmap that stays inside the new-tab purpose

The ratings below are my estimates for one to three engineers who know the codebase. **S** is up to a week, **M** two to four weeks, and **L** one to three months. Policy risk is graded against the CWS rules: Low means no new permission and a clear fit with "new tab page"; Medium means a new permission or a function a reviewer could read as separate; High means all-sites access, a restricted Google OAuth scope, or a clearly separate purpose. Google lets a new-tab extension include AI chatbots and vertical search. It also says the action button "cannot introduce any additional functionality or content", that "broad, multi-purpose toolbars are not allowed", and that permissions must be the narrowest possible ([CWS Quality Guidelines FAQ](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/quality-guidelines-faq); [CWS Program Policies](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/policies)). For this report I checked the current v1.3.0 manifest. It requests `storage`, `unlimitedStorage`, `notifications`, `tabs`, `idle`, `alarms`, `identity`, `contextMenus` and `webNavigation`; `scripting`, `tabCapture`, `offscreen` and `downloads` are optional; and `<all_urls>` is declared only as an optional host permission. The Time Tracker already redirects a tab when a limit is hit. That means most Tier 1 work needs **no new permission at all**.

### Tier 3: later, only if users ask for it

Several features have real demand but a poor fit right now. **Email-to-task from Gmail** is a proven habit (Todoist's Gmail extension has 100K users), but a Gmail sidebar needs host access to Gmail, and reading mail through the Gmail API means a restricted Google scope with a heavier security review. The existing right-click capture already covers the lightweight case. **Full-page screenshot and annotate** faces GoFullPage (11M, 4.9) as a free incumbent and reads as a separate function. **Kanban, table and Eisenhower views** are cheap views over existing data, but no source measures their use. **In-page distraction hiding** (YouTube Shorts and recommendations) needs per-site host access and breaks whenever those pages change. **Two-way calendar sync** needs a write scope and a new verification just as the read-only scope is being approved.

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| A generic "summarise / chat with this page" sidebar | Gemini in Chrome does it natively, and Claude (23M), Sider (5M) and Monica (3M) dominate the sidebar slot |
| Split view, reading mode, vertical tabs, tab grouping | Chrome ships all four (see next section) |
| AI auto-scheduling that rearranges the calendar | Clockwise shut down; Motion rates 4.1 with rigidity complaints; suites bundle scheduling free |
| Autonomous agents | Heavy marketing, low measured use; surveys put writing, search and summaries far ahead |
| Whiteboards, backlinks and graph views | Expensive to build, power-user niche, unmeasured use |
| Team workspaces, sharing and assigning | Team features draw complaints even at Todoist; ProdLi's buyer is an individual |
| A persistent highlighter or translation overlay on every site | Requires all-sites access, the highest-scrutiny permission, and duplicates Chrome and large incumbents |
| A meeting bot, or recording meetings the user is not attending | The single most-hated meeting-assistant behaviour |
| Changing the search provider | Google counts it as a separate function that cannot be bundled |

One policy question needs an answer before the store update. ProdLi's toolbar button is labelled "click on a Google Meet tab to record", so it starts a meeting recording. Google's FAQ says a new-tab extension's action button may only be a shortcut to the main feature ([CWS Quality Guidelines FAQ](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/quality-guidelines-faq)). I found no enforcement cases showing where Google draws that line for all-in-one new-tab extensions. Still, the listing and the button's behaviour should present meeting notes as feeding the new-tab hub, and the popup should never grow into a separate mini-app.

## Chrome and churn define what not to build

Chrome has absorbed several former extension categories. Gemini in Chrome launched on **18 September 2025** for US users on Mac and Windows with English set as the language. It explains pages, compares and summarises across tabs, and works with Calendar, YouTube and Maps, and AI Mode answers questions about the current page from the address bar ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/new-ai-features-for-chrome/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/google-brings-gemini-in-chrome-to-us-users-unveils-agentic-browsing-capabilities-and-more/?rand=324)). Later updates kept Gemini in a side panel across tabs and added "auto browse" ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/gemini-3-auto-browse/)), with full agentic browsing limited to paid tiers ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/google-is-rolling-out-the-biggest-changes-to-chrome-in-years-heres-whats-changing)). **Vertical tabs and a full-window reading mode** began rolling out on 7 April 2026 ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/new-chrome-productivity-features/)). Split view was announced in February 2026, according to a secondary source ([thetabextension.com](https://thetabextension.com/blog/best-browsers-tab-management-2026)). Saved tab groups sync across signed-in devices ([Supercharge Browser](https://www.superchargebrowser.com/library/chrome-tab-groups-complete-guide/)). The rule for ProdLi is to **persist, connect and keep private** what Chrome only shows in the moment. Gemini summarises a page and forgets it, while ProdLi files the summary into notes and links it to a task. Chrome groups live tabs, while ProdLi archives sessions with backups and export. Gemini's US-English limit means ProdLi's AI still fills a gap for many users elsewhere, but that is no reason to rebuild a generic page summariser.

The second constraint is how people abandon productivity apps. The research here is weak and mostly vendor-written, but the themes repeat. Users quit when keeping the system up feels heavier than the benefit ([ToolVerdicts](https://toolverdicts.blogspot.com/2026/01/why-you-stop-using-productivity-apps.html)). Feature bloat and poor onboarding drive early churn ([FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/why-users-stop-using-productivity-apps-and-how-to-fix-it)). Sync bugs and duplicate events top the complaints about AI calendars ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/reclaim-ai)). TickTick hides its extra modules by default, and reviewers then complain they are hard to find ([Murat Esmer](https://muratesmer.pages.dev/blog/ticktick-review)). The lesson is to keep modules off by default but easy to discover. Three design rules follow for ProdLi. First, every new module (blocking, sessions, read-later, richer habits) should start off and be offered in context, for example a blocking prompt on the third visit to a limited site. Second, reliability work should take priority over new features before each release, because data loss is the top category of low-star reviews for notes and tab managers alike. Third, the core should stay free, with only AI usage metered. Evernote shows that limits convert, and Momentum shows that paywalling new-tab basics breeds resentment.

## Conclusion

The evidence changes the question ProdLi should ask. It is not "which categories are we missing?" but "what brings someone back to their new tab tomorrow?" The behaviours with the best usage evidence each take a few seconds and recur daily: a reminder fires, a recurring task reappears, a site gets blocked at 9 a.m., a spoken sentence becomes a dated task. ProdLi has impressive surface area but lacks most of these hooks. A new-tab extension is already opened dozens of times a day, so recurring tasks, reminders and scheduled blocking surfaced on every tab are worth more than any new AI feature. They also need no new permissions, which makes them the safest content for a store update.

ProdLi's defensible position is the combination competitors cannot easily copy: no bots, a narrow set of permissions, everything connected on the new tab, and AI that works on the user's own data under clear limits. Each roadmap choice should be tested against that positioning, which rules out a page-summarising sidebar, an auto-scheduler, an all-sites highlighter and a team workspace. The biggest unknown is still per-feature demand among ProdLi's own users. A privacy-first product cannot fill that gap with tracking, so the practical substitutes are an in-app one-question poll after Tier 1 ships, close reading of store reviews, and a manual check of the State of Obsidian 2026 results, which could not be retrieved for this report.

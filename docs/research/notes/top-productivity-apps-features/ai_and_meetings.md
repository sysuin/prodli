# AI Meeting Assistants and AI Productivity Assistants: Most-Used Features (2025–2026), for ProdLi

Research date: 2026-11-18. Scope: Part A (meeting assistants) and Part B (AI productivity assistants, all-in-one demand). Evidence labels used below: **[USAGE]** = measured usage or telemetry; **[COMPANY]** = self-reported company figure (unaudited); **[SECONDARY]** = third-party estimate or re-report; **[REVIEW]** = reviewer or user opinion; **[MARKETING]** = product page claim.

## Q1. Which meeting assistants lead (users/rank, 2025–2026), and which features are standard?

### Takeaway
Platform-native note-taking (Google Meet "Take notes for me", Zoom AI Companion, Teams Copilot recap) has the largest reach, with Google reportedly at roughly 110M users of "Take notes for me". Among independents, Otter (35M+ users, $100M ARR) and Fireflies (about 20M users, $1B valuation) are largest. Granola, which records without a bot, is the fastest-rising: valued at a reported $1.5B in March 2026. The standard feature set is now: transcript + summary + action items, AI chat across all meetings, custom templates, automatic recap sharing, and CRM/Slack/Notion sync. Video clips, in-person/mobile capture and bot-free recording separate the products.

### Inferences
- **Table stakes in 2026** (every leader has them): summary + action items, AI chat across all meetings with citations, custom templates per meeting type, automatic recap sharing (email/Slack), searchable library. ProdLi already has most of this; the gaps are likely **templates per meeting type, citations/jump-to-transcript in chat, and automatic sharing**.
- **Bot-free capture is now the growth vector** (Granola, Notion, Krisp, Google native). ProdLi's caption-based no-bot capture is on-trend, but competitors capture device audio, so they cover **Zoom web, Teams web and in-person**. ProdLi covers Google Meet only. Extending tab-audio capture to Zoom/Teams browser clients would close the biggest coverage gap versus Granola/Notion.
- **Reusable prompts ("Recipes") and "Prep my day" briefings** match ProdLi's existing AI chat and daily brief. Adding saved and shareable prompt templates is low-cost and copies a proven Granola feature.
- **Pre-meeting prep** (pulling past meetings with the same attendees before a call) is where Zoom and Granola usage is expanding, which suggests demand beyond post-meeting summaries.
- Video clips and highlights (Fathom, Fireflies) are a sales/CS niche. They're probably lower priority for a personal productivity extension.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025–2026 user counts found for Read.ai, tl;dv, MeetGeek, Bluedot, Krisp (meeting notes) or Fathom. A comparison site lists Fathom as "1M+ users", but the figure is undated and unverified.
- Google's 110M "Take notes for me" figure was not checked against a Google primary page. Wording differs between sources ("used in a month" vs "have tried").
- No vendor publishes usage of individual features (for example the share of users who use cross-meeting chat vs templates). Feature prevalence is inferred from feature lists and launch cadence.
- Microsoft has not published Copilot-in-Teams user totals or feature rankings.
- Language-support counts per tool and Bluedot (a Chrome-extension, bot-free competitor and the closest analog to ProdLi) were not researched due to the tool-call budget.

## Q2. What do users praise, complain about, and request in meeting assistants?

### Takeaway
The dominant complaint is **bots joining meetings**: they appear as awkward "participants", join meetings the owner isn't attending, and hosts have to kick them or admins have to ban them. Privacy/consent is the next concern. Accuracy complaints focus on technical vocabulary and non-English speech. Praise centers on free or generous plans, easy sharing, and "minimal, distraction-free" bot-free tools.

### Cited Findings
- [REVIEW/forum] Zoom Community users ask how to block Otter, Read.ai and Fireflies notetakers. One says "every meeting we have I have to kick several of these AI notetakers". Bots sometimes join via a participant's authenticated account, which makes domain blocking ineffective. Workarounds: waiting rooms, or asking Zoom support to block bots. — [Zoom Community](https://community.zoom.com/t5/Zoom-Meetings/How-do-I-disable-AI-Notetakers-otter-ai-read-ai-fireflies-ai-etc/td-p/233848); [Zoom Community (Otter)](https://community.zoom.com/t5/Zoom-Meetings/How-to-stop-Otter-ai-Notetaker-from-joining-my-personal-meeting/m-p/238117); [Zoom Community (Supernormal)](https://community.zoom.com/t5/Zoom-Meetings/Supernormal-AI-Notetaker-keeps-joining-my-Zoom-calls/m-p/227721)
- [REVIEW] A Product Hunt thread calls it awkward when an AI bot joins a first client call as a third participant. Another user reports multiple notetaker bots in the participant list before the humans arrive. — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/general/am-i-the-only-one-who-finds-it-awkward-when-an-ai-notetaker-bot-joins-the-call-as-a-participant); [Product Hunt (bot hate)](https://www.producthunt.com/p/strikescribe-ai/does-anyone-else-hate-that-every-transcription-tool-now-sends-a-bot-to-join-your-call)
- [REVIEW, older: Sept 2024] An r/Journalism user complained that Otter's notetaker kept auto-joining Google Meet invites they weren't attending. — [Reddit mirror](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/Journalism/comments/1fecd4v/im_at_my_whits_end_with_otter)
- [REVIEW] A Substack author reports blanket bans on AI notetakers in several meetings they attended. — [Mission Bloom Substack](https://missionbloom.substack.com/p/to-bot-or-not-why-blocking-ai-meeting)
- [REVIEW] ClickUp users ask to stop the AI notetaker from introducing itself in meeting chat. — [ClickUp feedback](https://feedback.clickup.com/ai-notetaker/p/prevent-ai-notetaker-from-introducing-itself-in-meeting-chat)
- [REVIEW] Fast Company covers notetaker privacy. It advises asking whether a notetaker is present and notes that some US state laws govern voiceprints. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91571498/ai-notetaker-work-meetings-privacy-data)
- [REVIEW] Per Zapier: Otter struggles with technical language, tl;dv sometimes fails to join when its servers are busy, Fireflies' UI is cluttered, Fathom has quirks on Meet/Teams, Granola has no video playback, and Calendly's AI is English-only. Praise: Granola's "minimal, distraction-free interface", Fathom's free tier and easy sharing, tl;dv's generous free plan. — [Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-meeting-assistant/)
- [COMPANY/SECONDARY] Granola's growth has been attributed to users preferring discreet on-device capture over visible bots. — [EntrepreneurLoop](https://entrepreneurloop.com/granola-ai-meeting-notes-unicorn-series-c-2026/); [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/granola-raises-20-million-for-ai-powered-notetaking/)
- [MARKETING] Notion's and Krisp's pitches lead with "no bots". Third-party guides still note participants must be informed and consent. — [Notion](https://www.notion.com/product/ai-meeting-notes); [Dupple guide](https://dupple.com/learn/how-to-use-notion-ai-meeting-notes)

### Inferences
- ProdLi's no-bot capture is a real selling point and should lead its marketing. Add a lightweight **consent aid**: a one-click "I'm taking AI notes" chat message or an indicator. This addresses privacy concerns without the "bot introduces itself" annoyance.
- **Never auto-join unattended meetings.** Bot tools are hated for exactly that (Otter). ProdLi should record only when the user is present.
- Accuracy on jargon and mixed languages is a known weakness. ProdLi's Whisper fallback for mixed-language calls addresses it. A **custom vocabulary/glossary** (names, product terms) is a cheap, high-value addition.

### Gaps
- Few first-hand Reddit threads surfaced in search. Most complaint evidence is from Zoom Community and Product Hunt. No quantitative survey found on the share of people who object to bots.
- No systematic data on feature requests (for example vote counts on public roadmaps).

## Q3. Which AI features do people actually use daily in productivity assistants (Copilot, Gemini, ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity, Motion, Reclaim, Notion AI, Saner, Mem)?

### Takeaway
Across independent surveys and OpenAI's own usage study, the most-used AI features are **searching/researching information, drafting/rewriting text, and summarizing**. Planning, scheduling and agent-style "do it for me" tasks are used far less. Daily AI use at work is still a minority: about 10–13% of US employees in Gallup's 2025 data. Proactive briefings exist (ChatGPT Pulse, then Scheduled Tasks). Auto-scheduling calendars (Motion, Reclaim) are praised but criticized for sync bugs and a steep learning curve.

### Cited Findings
- [USAGE, 1.5M conversations] In OpenAI's NBER study (Sept 2025, consumer plans), about 75% of conversations are practical guidance, information-seeking, or writing. **Writing is the most common work task.** By intent, 49% of messages are "Asking" (advice/info), 40% "Doing" (drafting, planning, coding) and 11% "Expressing". About 30% of use is work-related. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-people-are-using-chatgpt/)
- [USAGE survey] Gallup Q3 2025 (n=23,068): 42% of AI-using employees use it to summarize information. Daily AI use rose only from 8% to 10%; a later quarter hit 13%. Gallup Q1 2026: about half of US employees use AI at work, and daily-plus-weekly use is a record 28%. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/nearly-half-of-us-workers-now-use-ai-on-the-job-but-most-arent-using-it-daily/); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/half-of-all-us-employees-now-use-artificial-intelligence-at-work-crossing-landmark-threshold-for-first-time-gallup-data-shows-daily-and-weekly-usage-hitting-all-time-high-of-28-percent-in-q1-2026-with-65-percent-feeling-positive-about-its-impact-on-productivity)
- [USAGE survey] Pythian/Sirkin Research (Nov 2025) top frequent tasks: research 66%, drafting/editing 64%, summarizing documents 43%, **finding documents across data sources 42%**. — [Pythian](https://www.pythian.com/blog/ai-in-the-workplace-employee-productivity-sentiment-survey)
- [USAGE survey] Verasight (July 2025, n=971) top work uses: research/background 24.5%, brainstorming 20.2%, summarizing 18.0%. About four in ten don't use AI at work. — [Verasight](https://data.verasight.io/ai/four-in-ten-americans-say-they-dont-use-ai-at-work)
- [USAGE survey] US Census Bureau: 55% of workers have used AI for at least one of 11 tasks. Most common: finding information, drafting communications/documentation, generating ideas, summarizing. — [LetsDataScience on Census](https://letsdatascience.com/news/census-data-tracks-ais-workplace-adoption-85fedf81)
- [USAGE survey, smaller vendor] AI Resume Builder (Apr 2025, n=1,000): more than half of AI users use it to write emails, draft content, brainstorm and summarize. — [MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2025/53323/how-people-are-using-ai-at-work)
- [USAGE signal] Microsoft internally reports "Rewrite with Copilot" in Teams as one of the most-used features, and Microsoft elevated meeting recap to a headline Copilot metric. Zoom says AI Companion usage expanded to meeting prep and post-meeting task management. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/activity-reports/microsoft-365-copilot-usage); [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/zoom-delivers-strong-q2-sees-ai-usage-surge)
- [COMPANY] ChatGPT Tasks (Jan 2025) handles scheduled reminders and actions. **ChatGPT Pulse** (Sept 25, 2025, Pro, mobile) researched overnight from memory, chat history and connected apps such as the calendar, and delivered a morning briefing. Pulse was **sunset in June 2026** and folded into "Scheduled tasks", available on Go/Plus/Pro/Business/Enterprise. — [OpenAI Pulse](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-pulse/); [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/articles/12293630-chatgpt-pulse); [Gigazine Jun 2026](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260619-chatgpt-scheduled-tasks/)
- [REVIEW] Reclaim.ai is rated about 4.8/5 on G2 (141 reviews): praised for easy scheduling and adapting to changed priorities, criticized for calendar sync issues, duplicated events and rescheduling glitches. Motion is rated 4.1/5 on G2: praised for heavy-calendar auto-scheduling, criticized for a steep learning curve, rigidity when plans change, a 7-day trial and its price (about $49/month per a Saner review). — [G2 Reclaim](https://www.g2.com/products/reclaim-ai); [G2 compare](https://www.g2.com/compare/motionapp-vs-reclaim-ai-vs-calendly); [Capterra Motion](https://www.capterra.com/p/214264/Motion/reviews/); [Saner.ai Motion review](https://blog.saner.ai/motion-reviews)
- [MARKETING] Notion bundles AI meeting notes, Notion Mail (drafting, inbox organization) and Notion Calendar. Free plan includes Mail/Calendar; meeting notes require Business+. — [Techfinitive](https://techfinitive.com/notion-unveils-ai-tools-to-boost-work-and-email); [Notion help](https://www.notion.com/sv/help/ai-meeting-notes)

### Inferences
- ProdLi's existing AI set (rewrite selected text, chat over notes/tasks/meetings, summaries, daily brief) maps well onto the top three measured uses: **drafting/rewriting, search/Q&A, summarizing**. The highest-value additions are probably ones that extend these:
  1. **Summarize any web page/tab/selection** into a note. A Chrome extension can do this where the content lives, and summarizing is a top-3 use.
  2. **Search across more sources** in AI chat (bookmarks, saved pages, Gmail/Calendar). "Finding documents across data sources" is 42% in Pythian.
  3. **AI email drafting/reply anywhere** (Gmail compose). Writing is OpenAI's #1 work task, and Copilot Rewrite is heavily used.
- **Scheduled/proactive briefings** are converging on "scheduled tasks" (OpenAI killed the standalone Pulse). ProdLi's daily brief could gain user-defined scheduled prompts ("every Friday, summarize meetings with client X").
- **Auto-scheduling** (Motion/Reclaim) is a heavier, buggier category with lower satisfaction (Motion 4.1). Lightweight suggestions ("time-block this task?") are lower risk than full auto-rescheduling for a small team.
- Agent features (doing tasks for you) have high marketing presence, but surveys show low reported usage. Don't prioritize them.

### Gaps
- No usage data found for Saner.ai, Mem, Perplexity Comet/browser features, Claude desktop/projects/memory, or Gemini in Workspace beyond Meet. Not researched in depth due to the tool-call budget.
- No independent data on daily use of memory, projects, or agent/browser-automation features.
- Motion and Reclaim user counts were not found.
- Email-triage usage rates (as opposed to drafting) were not found.

## Q4. Is there evidence of tool sprawl and demand for all-in-one apps, and why do people abandon productivity apps?

### Takeaway
Vendor surveys consistently report heavy app-switching: about 33 switches per day, about 51 minutes per week lost, and about 1 hour per day searching for information. Microsoft telemetry shows an interruption every 2 minutes. That supports demand for consolidation. Evidence on why people abandon productivity apps is weak (mostly vendor blogs). Recurring themes: upkeep feels heavier than the benefit, feature bloat, poor onboarding, sync and reliability bugs, and tools that don't fit an existing daily workflow.

### Cited Findings
- [USAGE, Microsoft 365 telemetry + survey of 31K workers in 31 countries, 2025] Employees are interrupted every 2 minutes during core hours by meetings, emails or pings, about 275 times a day. Averages: 117 emails and 153 Teams messages daily. 60% of meetings are ad hoc. 48% of employees and 52% of leaders say work feels chaotic and fragmented. — [Microsoft WorkLab: Infinite Workday](https://microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday); [Microsoft WTI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born)
- [Survey, vendor] Lokalise (Sept 2025, 1,000 US white-collar workers): workers switch between tabs/apps/platforms 33 times a day on average (17% more than 100 times), lose 51 minutes per week (44 hours per year) to "tool fatigue", and 56% say it hurts their work weekly. Secondary claim (unverified): 79% say their company has done nothing to consolidate. — [Lokalise](https://lokalise.com/blog/blog-tool-fatigue-productivity-report/); [Yu-kai Chou](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/ai-tool-sprawl/)
- [Survey, vendor, older: 2021] Qatalog + Cornell Ellis Idea Lab: workers spend nearly an hour a day looking for information across apps, and 45% say context-switching hurts productivity. — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/app-switching-enterprise-productivity-software-qatalog/602281/)
- [Survey, vendor, Dec 2025] Buddy Punch (500+ US ops leaders): 82% say tool overload harms efficiency. — [Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/business-economy/invisible-workplace-problem-stealing-hours)
- [Opinion/estimate] Freshworks' "Frankenstacks" report quotes an executive estimating 7 hours a week lost to context-switching. — [CX Today](https://www.cxtoday.com/?p=81243)
- [Vendor blog, unsourced numbers] Productivity apps reportedly retain 17.1% of users on Day 1 and 4.1% by Day 30. Causes cited: weak onboarding and feature bloat. — [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/why-users-stop-using-productivity-apps-and-how-to-fix-it)
- [Vendor, subscription apps generally] 37% of cancellations happen because people stop using the product, and about 35% of churners cite value concerns. — [Airbridge](https://airbridge.io/blog/why-subscription-churn-happens-top-5-cancellation-reasons-forecast-for-mobile-apps-in-2026)
- [Opinion] People quit when maintaining the system feels heavier than avoiding it. Tools stick when they fit an existing daily pain rather than requiring a new habit. — [ToolVerdicts](https://toolverdicts.blogspot.com/2026/01/why-you-stop-using-productivity-apps.html); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/fe5752e9f7)
- [REVIEW] Sync bugs, duplicate events and a learning curve are the top complaints for AI calendars (Reclaim, Motion). — [G2 Reclaim](https://www.g2.com/products/reclaim-ai); [Capterra Motion](https://www.capterra.com/p/214264/Motion/reviews/)
- [Market signal] Notion is bundling meeting notes, mail and calendar. Otter positions itself as a "knowledge base". Granola is expanding into team "Spaces" and an enterprise context API. ### Inferences
- The case for all-in-one rests mainly on **less switching and searching**, not on feature count. ProdLi's pitch should be "one place where your meetings, notes and tasks are already connected and searchable". Feature bloat is itself a top abandonment cause, so new features should hide behind progressive disclosure.
- Abandonment themes point to priorities: **near-zero setup** (auto-capture Meet calls, auto-create tasks from action items), **reliability over breadth** (sync bugs kill AI calendars), and **fitting existing habits** (living in the browser where work happens is a structural advantage for a Chrome extension).
- Easy export and import (Notion, Google Docs, Todoist) may reduce the perceived risk of switching. This is not directly evidenced in the sources found.

# Most-used features in leading task-management, daily-planning and calendar/time-blocking apps (2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-11-18. Sources are mostly 2025–2026. Older items are flagged. Each item is labeled as **[usage evidence]**, **[company claim]**, **[reviewer opinion]**, or **[marketing/feature list]**. Most review sites in this space (Morgen, Akiflow, Reclaim, Saner, Ellie blogs) are competitors writing comparisons, so treat their verdicts as opinion.

## 1. For each app: user numbers or rank (2025–2026), and the features users rely on most

### Takeaway
Hard usage data is scarce. Todoist is the only app with fresh, specific numbers: 50M+ users claimed in January 2026, 500K Chrome extension users, and published adoption stats for its AI voice capture. The most consistent "core" in reviewer lists is fast capture (natural-language quick add), due dates and reminders, lists/projects/labels, cross-device sync, and calendar integration. TickTick is the reference app for bundling a calendar, Pomodoro, habits and an Eisenhower matrix. Sunsama and Akiflow define the "daily planning ritual + drag-to-timebox" category. Motion and Reclaim define AI auto-scheduling.

### Inferences
- Chrome Web Store counts are the most comparable usage signal for a Chrome-extension team: Todoist 500K (main) + 100K (Gmail), TickTick 100K. Gmail-embedded task capture alone draws 100K users for Todoist, which suggests "turn an email into a task" is a real habit.
- Feature breadth does not predict popularity. Todoist (no built-in Pomodoro or habits) leads on users and reviews, while TickTick wins on bundled extras and price. Speed of capture and organizing appears to matter more than feature count.
- The AI-calendar category is unstable. Clockwise shut down, Reclaim was absorbed by Dropbox, Amie shifted to meeting notes, and Motion moved upmarket to "AI employees". Standalone AI auto-scheduling for individuals is a crowded, churning niche, while suites (Notion, Microsoft, Google) bundle scheduling for free.
- Free defaults (Google Tasks, Microsoft To Do, Apple Reminders) win on embedding in the email and calendar people already use, despite being "barebones".

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 user numbers were found for TickTick, Things 3, Any.do, Akiflow, Sunsama, Morgen, Fantastical, Structured, Amie or Notion Calendar (standalone). Microsoft To Do and Google Tasks have no separate public counts.
- Asana, ClickUp and Trello for personal use were not researched within the tool budget. No data on which of their features individuals use.
- Morgen, Fantastical and Structured were not researched in depth. Feature and usage data for them is missing here.
- No vendor publishes per-feature usage shares (for example, "X% of users use recurring tasks"). The only per-feature usage data found is Todoist Ramble's beta stats (section 2).
- Google Tasks Chrome extension user counts were not visible in the search results.

## 2. Which AI features shipped in 2025–2026 and are actually used?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced AI feature is AI capture: turning speech or messy text into structured tasks. Todoist Ramble has real adoption data, and Doist reports a roughly 5x upgrade lift. AI auto-scheduling (Motion, Reclaim) is the most-marketed feature but has no public per-feature usage data, and its standalone vendors are consolidating or pivoting. Task breakdown and subtask suggestions are now common checkbox features (Google Tasks via Gemini, Any.do, Microsoft Copilot) with no usage evidence. Zapier explicitly does not weigh AI when ranking to-do apps.

### Cited Findings
- [usage evidence] Todoist Ramble (voice to structured tasks, including project, date, deadline, priority, duration and assignee, with mid-sentence corrections such as "Actually, make that Thursday"):
  - Beta-tested with about 150,000 Todoist Experimental users. About 76,000 testers completed about 290,000 sessions in the first three weeks.
  - Public beta in November 2025. Generally available January 21, 2026 on all platforms, in 38 languages.
  - Task-creation success rose from about 40% (October) to about 62% (December).
  - Doist claims new entry-level users upgraded at about 5x the previous rate after using Ramble.
  - Free plan gets limited sessions per month; Pro and Business are unlimited.
  - Built on Gemini 2.5 Flash Live via Vertex AI, as part of "Todoist Assist".
  - — [TechCrunch, Jan 21 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/todoists-app-now-lets-you-add-tasks-to-your-to-do-list-by-speaking-to-its-ai/)
- [marketing/feature list] Google Tasks has Gemini integration to add tasks or break them down. Any.do has built-in AI subtask suggestions. — [Zapier, Dec 2025](https://zapier.com/blog/best-to-do-list-apps/)
- [product change] Microsoft Planner's Copilot "Project Manager agent" (Feb 2025) generates and runs tasks. In September 2025 it came to Teams meetings, turning meeting conversations into tasks. Requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license. — [Microsoft Planner blog, Feb 2025](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/plannerblog/whats-new-in-microsoft-planner---february-2025/4386912); [Sept 2025](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/plannerblog/what%E2%80%99s-new-in-microsoft-planner-%E2%80%93-september-2025/4458181)
- [company/analyst] Motion's 2025 growth driver was "AI Employees" (autonomous agents), not the original auto-scheduler. AI Agents added in 2025 extend into proactive scheduling. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/motion); [Morgen, Motion review](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-review)
- [reviewer opinion] Neither Sunsama nor Akiflow does what most people mean by "AI scheduling" in 2026. Their AI assistants are light. — [Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/sunsama-vs-akiflow)
- [reviewer opinion] Zapier on its evaluation method: "No app made this list just because it had great AI scheduling or could automatically suggest sub-tasks." It calls Apple Intelligence in Reminders "more miss than hit" and says Microsoft To Do "hasn't quite caught up with Microsoft's AI pivot". — [Zapier, Dec 2025](https://zapier.com/blog/best-to-do-list-apps/)
- [reviewer opinion] Motion has no suggest-then-approve mode, so the AI takes full control of your calendar. — [Morgen, Motion review](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-review)

### Inferences
- For a Chrome extension, the most evidence-backed AI investment is AI or natural-language capture: free-text or voice to a task with date, priority and project. It builds on the most-used core behavior (quick add) and has measured adoption and monetization lift.
- AI auto-scheduling is a differentiator for a niche of power users. Its failure mode, lost control, is a known complaint, so a "suggest a plan, user approves" design is safer than full autonomy.
- Task breakdown ("split into subtasks") is cheap to add with an LLM and now common. Expect low differentiation.

### Gaps
- No independent usage data for Motion or Reclaim auto-scheduling (for example, share of users who keep auto-scheduling turned on).
- No usage figures for Gemini in Google Tasks, Any.do AI subtasks, TickTick AI features, Sunsama's "Sunny" or Akiflow's AI assistant.
- The full Todoist Assist feature set beyond Ramble (for example, AI task breakdown or filters) was not confirmed from a primary 2025–2026 source.

## 3. What do users most often praise, complain about, or request?

### Takeaway
Praise centers on fast capture, reliable cross-device sync, clean and uncluttered UI, and bundled extras at a low price (TickTick). Complaints center on price and paywalled basics (Todoist filters and labels, TickTick calendar view), cluttered desktop UIs, weak team features, slow support, and lack of control or data portability with AI and calendar tools (Motion's full autonomy, Clockwise's shutdown). Direct Reddit threads could not be retrieved, so this section rests on reviews (Zapier, TechRadar, Capterra, AlternativeTo).

### Inferences
- An extension that gives free calendar view, labels and filters, and a daily planning flow would address the most-cited paywall complaints of Todoist, TickTick and Sunsama users.

- Local or exportable data and "approve before scheduling" controls answer trust concerns raised by the AI-calendar churn.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads (r/productivity, r/todoist, r/ticktick) were retrieved. Searches returned aggregators only, so ranked "most requested feature" lists from Reddit or vendor forums (Todoist Ideas, TickTick feature requests) are missing.

- G2 star ratings and review counts per app were not captured.

## 4. Which features are table stakes vs. differentiators?

### Takeaway
Table stakes (nearly every app; the minimum for a credible extension): fast quick add with natural-language dates, due dates and reminders, recurring tasks, lists/projects with tags or labels and priorities, subtasks, a Today/Inbox view, cross-device sync, and Google/Outlook calendar visibility. Common but not universal: calendar view, drag-to-timebox, Gmail/Outlook email-to-task, sharing and assigning, kanban. Differentiators: built-in Pomodoro, habit tracker and Eisenhower matrix (TickTick), a guided daily planning and shutdown ritual (Sunsama, Any.do "Plan my Day", Akiflow), a universal inbox pulling from Slack, Gmail and others (Akiflow), AI auto-scheduling (Motion, Reclaim), AI voice capture (Todoist Ramble), and karma/streaks or heatmaps.

### Cited Findings
- [reviewer criteria] Zapier's must-haves for a to-do app: "Make it fast to add and organize tasks", "Offer multiple ways to organize your tasks", "Remind you about self-imposed deadlines", "Offer clean user interfaces", "Sync between every platform you use." Apps without desktop-mobile sync were excluded. — [Zapier, Dec 2025](https://zapier.com/blog/best-to-do-list-apps/)
- [feature lists] Across Zapier's seven picks:
  - Due dates and subtasks appear in nearly all.
  - Natural-language entry: Todoist, TickTick.
  - Calendar integration: TickTick, Things, Any.do, Reminders, Google Tasks.
  - Email-to-task: Todoist Email Assist, Microsoft To Do flagged emails, Google Tasks drag-from-Gmail.
  - Sharing: Reminders, Todoist.
  - Daily planning: Any.do.
  - Pomodoro, habits and Eisenhower matrix: TickTick only.
  - — [Zapier, Dec 2025](https://zapier.com/blog/best-to-do-list-apps/)
- [reviewer opinion] Kanban and timeline views separate TickTick from Google Tasks. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/reviews/ticktick)
- [feature lists] Daily-planning apps share these features: drag tasks onto the calendar to timebox, estimate durations, a Today/Week view next to the calendar, and an end-of-day shutdown or reflection (Sunsama, Akiflow). — [Ellie Planner](https://ellieplanner.com/comparisons/sunsama-vs-akiflow); [The Business Dive](https://thebusinessdive.com/akiflow-vs-sunsama)
- [feature lists] AI auto-scheduling across Google, Outlook and iCloud plus email or voice capture (Motion), and Outlook support added August 2025 (Reclaim). — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/motion); [Morgen](https://morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-vs-reclaim)
- [feature lists] Widgets and lock-screen capture: Todoist Ramble ships iOS Home/Lock Screen shortcuts and Android widgets and quick-settings tiles. TickTick has an interactive matrix/calendar widget. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/todoists-app-now-lets-you-add-tasks-to-your-to-do-list-by-speaking-to-its-ai/); [App Store, TickTick](https://apps.apple.com/app/ticktick-todo-task-list/id626144601)
- [feature lists] Heatmap and streak-style visualization: TickTick added a yearly heatmap in early 2026. — [Murat Esmer review](https://muratesmer.pages.dev/blog/ticktick-review)

### Inferences (applied to ProdLi's current base: to-dos with priorities, due dates and subtasks; daily brief; simple Google Calendar time blocks)

How each feature maps onto ProdLi:
- **Table-stakes gaps to close first, if missing:**
  - Natural-language quick add (with a global keyboard shortcut or omnibox entry, the extension equivalent of Things' Quick Entry).
  - Recurring tasks.
  - Reminders/notifications.
  - Labels/projects plus saved filters.
  - An Inbox → Today triage flow.
  - Free tags and filters would counter the paywall complaints aimed at Todoist.
- **Email-to-task from Gmail:** high-leverage for a Chrome extension. Todoist's Gmail extension alone has 100K users, and Google Tasks and Microsoft To Do lean on email capture.
- **Drag-to-timebox, and a calendar view with tasks beside the day:** ProdLi already has simple time blocks, so upgrading to drag-and-drop timeboxing with duration estimates is the natural next step. It is the core loop of Sunsama and Akiflow.
- **Guided daily planning ritual and shutdown:** pairs naturally with the existing daily brief. It is Sunsama's core differentiator and Any.do's "Plan my Day", and Sunsama charges about $20+/month for it.
- **Mid-tier differentiators, cheap to add as opt-in modules:**
  - Pomodoro/focus timer.
  - Eisenhower matrix view (priority × due date already exist, so it is a view over existing data).
  - Habit list.
  - Streaks or heatmap.
  - TickTick shows these bundle well if hidden by default.
- **AI to prioritize:**
  1. AI/natural-language capture: best usage evidence.
  2. "Suggest a plan for today," which the user approves before it lands on the calendar. This avoids Motion's full-autonomy complaint.
  3. Task breakdown: common, low differentiation.
  - Full autonomous auto-scheduling is expensive and in a consolidating market.
- **Lower priority for a personal extension:** kanban boards, sharing and assigning, and team collaboration (team features draw complaints even in Todoist). Also a universal inbox pulling from Slack and others (Akiflow's niche, and integration-heavy).

- Karma and streaks (Todoist Karma) usage was not covered by any 2025–2026 source found.

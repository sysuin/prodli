# Most installed browser extensions (Chrome, Edge, Firefox) and what an all-in-one productivity extension could bring in — fact base as of 23 Nov 2026

Method note: Chrome Web Store (CWS) figures below were pulled directly from each live listing page on 23 Nov 2026 (user count, star rating, rating count, "Updated" date). CWS rounds users to buckets and caps display; counts are a proxy for active installs, not downloads. Firefox figures are from the official addons.mozilla.org (AMO) API ("average daily users"), Edge figures from the Edge Add-ons product API ("activeInstallCount"), both pulled the same day. Rows marked "?" are listings where the page rendered without a parsable user count.

## 1. Which extensions have the most users (10M+, 1M+ tiers) in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
On Chrome the 10M+ tier is dominated by PDF (Adobe Acrobat, 352M, mostly bundled/preinstalled), ad blockers (AdBlock 65M, Adblock Plus 41M, uBlock Origin Lite 23M), Grammarly (41M), Google Translate (39M), enterprise/education plugins, and, new in 2026, the official Claude extension (24M); GoFullPage (11M) is the only pure productivity utility above 10M. The 1M–10M tier is where productivity lives: screen recorders (Screencastify 9M, Loom 8M), Google Keep (8M), password managers (7–8M), Dark Reader (7M), Read Aloud and QuillBot (6M each), Sider (5M), and screenshot/translate/new-tab tools at 2–4M.

## 2. Which categories dominate installs, and which are rising in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Installs are dominated by ad/content blocking, PDF, writing assistance, translation, security/VPN, and admin-pushed enterprise/education tools. Rising in 2025–26: AI assistants and agents (Claude 24M, Sider 5M, Monica 3M), AI-flavoured writing (QuillBot 6M), AI translation (DeepL 4M, Immersive Translate 3M), and botless meeting notes (Tactiq 1M at 4.9). Classic tab managers, suspenders and focus blockers are flat or shrinking, and Gemini built into Chrome now competes directly with AI sidebars.

### Cited Findings
- Ad blockers hold 4 of the top ~16 Chrome slots (AdBlock 66M, Adblock Plus 41M, uBlock Origin Lite 23M, AdGuard 17M) — [chrome-stats.com top](https://chrome-stats.com/top)
- On Firefox, 7 of the top 12 are privacy/ad-blocking; uBlock Origin alone has 11.7M daily users — [AMO API](https://addons.mozilla.org/api/v5/addons/search/?sort=users&type=extension&page_size=30&app=firefox&lang=en-US)
- Claude's listing describes itself as "what lets Claude see and act in your browser", directed from the side panel or the Claude desktop app — [CWS Claude listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fcoeoabgfenejglbffodgkkbkcdhcgfn)
- Sider is described by reviewers as the most-installed multi-model sidebar (~5M installs, 114K reviews); Monica ~3M; MaxAI positioned on quick actions with a limited free tier (one source: 10 content chats/day, 40 AI responses/day) — [zplatform.ai](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/ai-chrome-extensions/), [prophetchrome.com](https://prophetchrome.com/best-ai-sidebar-extensions) (affiliate/vendor-adjacent sources)
- Trending report: "Codex for Chrome ChatGPT Sidebar" at 30K+ users with 1,412 new ratings in 30 days — [appgazers.com trending](https://appgazers.com/reports/chrome-extensions/trending)
- Gemini in Chrome launched to US users in Sept 2025 with multi-tab reasoning (compare/summarize across tabs) and agentic browsing — [TechCrunch, Sept 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/google-brings-gemini-in-chrome-to-us-users-unveils-agentic-browsing-capabilities-and-more/)
- Jan 2026: Chrome moved Gemini from a floating window into a sidebar, integrated Gemini 3 and added "auto browse" agent features; the agent asks for user intervention for sign-in/purchases — [TechCrunch, Jan 2026](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/chrome-takes-on-ai-browsers-with-tighter-gemini-integration-agentic-features-for-autonomous-tasks/), [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/gemini-3-auto-browse/?hl=sv)
- Features optional; aimed at US macOS, Windows and Chromebook Plus users — [BGR](https://bgr.com/2087015/gemini-in-chrome-2026-features-auto-browse); later rolling out in Malaysia (April 2026) — [Google Malaysia blog](https://malaysia.googleblog.com/)
- Meeting notes: Tactiq 1M, 4.9 stars (4.7K), updated 20 Nov 2026; Otter.ai's extension 300K and markets "No bot in Google Meet" — [Tactiq CWS](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fggkaccpbmombhnjkjokndojfgagejfb), [Otter CWS](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/bnmojkbbkkonlmlfgejehefjldooiedp)
- Shopping/coupon category damaged: Honey 2.2 stars on 179.6K ratings; exposé alleged affiliate-link hijacking and withheld coupons; class action filed — [Fortune, Dec 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/12/23/honey-extension-scam-drama/), [9to5Google Dec 2025](https://9to5google.com/2025/12/31/honey-chrome-users-fraud/)
- Tab tools look flat: OneTab 2M (3.8), Session Buddy 1M (3.3, last updated Apr 2026), Toby 300K, Workona 200K, The Marvellous Suspender 90K — live CWS listings in table above.
- Writing tools: Grammarly 41M, QuillBot 6M (4.7), LanguageTool 2M, Wordtune 1M (last updated Apr 2026) — live CWS listings in table above.

### Inferences
- The "rising" story for 2025–26 is AI everywhere: assistants (Claude, Sider, Monica, Merlin, HARPA), AI translation and AI writing. Sider's and Monica's very high rating counts relative to users suggest aggressive in-app review prompting.
- Gemini in Chrome (free, built in, multi-tab aware, sidebar) erodes the value of generic "chat with this page" sidebars. Differentiation now comes from the AI knowing the user's own data (tasks, notes, meetings), which ProdLi's Ask AI already does.
- Meeting notes are the productivity category with the best quality-to-size signal (Tactiq 4.9 stars at 1M). ProdLi already competes here.
- Tab management and tab suspension are partly absorbed by Chrome built-ins (tab groups and Memory Saver, from general knowledge, not verified this session), which helps explain the flat counts.

### Gaps
- No reliable time series of install growth by category for 2025–26 was found (chrome-stats history is paywalled). "Rising" is inferred from current counts plus older roundup figures, not measured growth.
- No source quantified how much Gemini in Chrome has cut third-party AI sidebar installs.

## 4. Which features fit ProdLi and which are a bad fit?

### Takeaway
Best fits: tab session save/restore, text snippets/expander, full-page and area screenshot with annotate-to-note, selection actions (translate, define, read aloud, summarize-to-note), web clipper/highlighter into Notes, and lightweight screen/tab recording. Dark mode, video speed, PDF editing, ad blocking, coupons and password management are poor fits: they are permission-heavy, security-sensitive, saturated by free tools, or off-purpose.

### Cited Findings
- ProdLi's current source manifest (`artifacts/prodli/extension/manifest.json`, read 23 Nov 2026) is MV3 with permissions `storage, unlimitedStorage, notifications, tabs, idle, alarms, identity, contextMenus, webNavigation, search, activeTab, scripting`, optional `tabCapture, offscreen, downloads`, optional hosts `https://meet.google.com/*` and `file:///*`, **no** always-on host permissions or content scripts, a `newtab` override, and commands for record/mark moment/quick add (local file, not a web source).

### Inferences
- Features that work through user gestures (`activeTab` + `scripting`, context menus, keyboard commands, side panel) fit ProdLi's current no-host-permission posture and add no install-time warnings: screenshot, clipper, selection actions, read aloud, AI summarize-to-note, tab sessions (`tabs` already granted).
- Features that must run on every page without a gesture (inline text expansion, dark mode, video speed, grammar underlines, inline translate bubbles) need `<all_urls>` and the "Read and change all your data on all websites" warning. Adding that as a required permission would disable ProdLi for existing users until they re-accept, and hurts review and trust. If done at all, use `optional_host_permissions` requested at the moment of use.
- The strongest storyline: "capture anything into your notes and tasks" (screenshot, clip, highlight, tab session, recording, summary). These map onto ProdLi's existing notes, tasks, sync and AI stack rather than bolting on unrelated utilities.
- Read aloud is a natural pair with existing voice dictation (input + output voice) and uses the warning-free `tts` permission.

- No usage data from ProdLi itself on which capture features users want; prioritization above is market-signal based.
- Whether Chrome's built-in reading-mode read-aloud, Memory Saver and Lens/screenshot tools reduce demand for those features was not quantified this session.

## 5. Chrome Web Store policy considerations (permissions, single purpose, MV3)

### Takeaway
An all-in-one extension is allowed if every feature serves one narrow, easy-to-state purpose (for ProdLi: "personal productivity workspace / new tab page"). Google names "toolbars that provide a broad array of functionality" as a violation, so bolting on unrelated utilities (dark mode, ad blocking, coupons, video speed) is the real single-purpose risk. MV3 bans remotely hosted code, and broad host permissions trigger the scariest warning and extra review.

### Cited Findings
- Policy text: "An extension must have a single purpose that is narrow and easy to understand." / "Don't create an extension that requires users to accept bundles of unrelated functionality." / clearly separate functions "should be put into two different extensions" — [CWS Quality Guidelines](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/quality-guidelines)
- Named violations include "Toolbars that provide a broad array of functionality or entry points into services", "Email notifiers combined with a news aggregator", and "New Tab Page extensions that alter the user's web search experience and don't respect the user's existing search settings" — [CWS Quality Guidelines](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/quality-guidelines)
- FAQ: single purpose can be a narrow focus area (e.g. "news headlines", "weather", "comparison shopping") or a narrow browser function (e.g. "new tab page", "tab management", "web search provider"); multi-feature extensions get "particular scrutiny" and every feature must be directly related to the single purpose; a new tab page offering AI chatbots or searching open tabs is given as acceptable; reviewers assume an extension uses every permission it requests; violators "will be removed" — [CWS Quality Guidelines FAQ](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/quality-guidelines-faq)
- MV3 bans remotely hosted code: all logic must ship in the package; remote data is fine; sandboxed iframes are an exception without page DOM access — [Chrome: Improving security in MV3](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/migrate/improve-security), [CWS MV3 requirements](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/mv3-requirements?hl=fr)
- Full uBlock Origin could not comply with MV3; uBlock Origin Lite replaced it on Chrome, while full uBO continues on Firefox and Edge — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/browsing/google-pulls-the-plug-on-ublock-origin), [The Register, Feb 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/07/google_chrome_extensions/). Edge still lists uBlock Origin at 15.0M installs — [Edge API](https://microsoftedge.microsoft.com/addons/getproductdetailsbycrxid/odfafepnkmbhccpbejgmiehpchacaeak)
- AdGuard was reportedly rejected five times for remote-code policy violations — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/07/google_chrome_extensions/)
- Affiliate Ads policy tightened 11 Mar 2025, enforced from 10 Jun 2025: affiliate links/codes/cookies only with disclosure, an explicit user action, and a direct user benefit (e.g. no affiliate link if no coupon is found) — [Chrome developer blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/cws-policy-update-affiliate-ads-2025)
- Permission-warning mechanics and texts — see section 4 ([permissions list](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/permissions-list), [permission warnings](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/permission-warnings))

### Inferences
- ProdLi's defensible single purpose is "a productivity new tab and workspace": tasks, notes, focus, meetings and AI over that data. Capture features (screenshot, clip, tab sessions, read-aloud of saved content) are "directly related" because their output lands in that workspace. Pure page-modification utilities (dark mode, video speed, ad blocking, volume boost) are not, and invite a "broad toolbar" rejection.
- The FAQ explicitly blesses a new tab page with AI chat and open-tab search, which supports ProdLi's new-tab + Ask AI + tab sessions combination.
- Listing copy should frame every feature around the single purpose (e.g. "save tabs, screenshots and clips into your ProdLi notes"), not as a feature grab-bag.
- MV3 constraints that matter for ProdLi additions: no remote code (AI prompts and models stay server-side as data/API calls, fine); service-worker lifetimes mean long recordings and TTS need `offscreen` documents (ProdLi already declares optional `offscreen`); inline per-page features need content scripts and host access.
- Keeping new capabilities behind optional permissions (as ProdLi already does for tabCapture/Meet/file://) avoids update-time disabling and keeps the install warning list short (per the permissions list, currently "Read your browsing history" from `tabs` and "Display notifications" from `notifications`; `identity`, `storage`, `contextMenus`, `scripting` carry no warning; `webNavigation`, `idle`, `search` warnings were not checked this session).
- The Great Suspender's removal for malware (2021, general knowledge, not re-verified here) and the Honey scandal are cautionary tales: in this category, trust and minimal permissions are a selling point.

### Gaps
- Google publishes no list of approved "all-in-one productivity" extensions, and no public enforcement data on single-purpose rejections for multi-feature productivity suites was found.
- Did not verify current CWS review-time differences for extensions requesting `<all_urls>` vs `activeTab` (widely reported anecdotally as longer, no primary source retrieved).
- Edge Add-ons and AMO single-purpose policies were not reviewed; AMO has no strict single-purpose rule as far as known, not verified this session.

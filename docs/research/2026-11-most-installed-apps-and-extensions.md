# Borrow the capture tools, skip the utilities

The most installed software on every platform is single-job utilities and AI assistants, not productivity suites. On Chrome, Edge and Firefox the top tier is ad blockers, PDF tools, Grammarly, translation, password managers and, new in 2026, the Claude extension at **24M Chrome users**. On phones, **ChatGPT was the world's most downloaded app of 2025 at 845M**, and the only "productivity" apps that reach the global and Indian charts are AI chatbots, PDF readers, scanners and authenticators. On the Mac, the AI coding tools and Claude's desktop app lead Homebrew installs, followed by free menu-bar utilities (Stats, Raycast, Rectangle, Maccy). On Windows, Microsoft's own PowerToys and Snipping Tool cover most power-user needs. Very little of this belongs in ProdLi. Ad blocking, coupons, passwords, dark mode, PDF editing, launchers and window managers are off-purpose, permission-heavy or already free in the OS. The features that do fit share one shape: **capture something and drop it into tasks or notes**. That means page and screen screenshots with OCR, read-aloud for saved pages, a menu-bar Pomodoro with keep-awake, a palette that takes typed prefixes, and, on the phone, habit-streak widgets, share-sheet capture and document scanning. A check of the code also changes the plan's starting point. ProdLi 1.4.0 already ships saved tab sessions, Read later with page summaries, focus blocking with a pause screen, voice-to-tasks, Ask sources, scheduled prompts, a drag-to-plan Day view, a Ctrl/Cmd+K palette, desktop per-app time tracking and habit streaks. Several Tier 1 and Tier 2 items are done, and the next weeks should go to trust, conversion and the cheap capture wins below.

## People install one-job tools, and OS vendors keep absorbing them

The reasons people install these tools fall into three jobs. **Capture** covers screenshots, recordings, clips and saved tabs. **Consume faster** covers read-aloud, translation, faster video playback and dark mode. **Control attention** covers blockers, new-tab pages and timers. ProdLi is already strong on control. Capture is the job that fits it best, because anything captured can land in its notes and tasks. Consume-faster tools mostly modify pages, which ProdLi should not do.

Ratings show what users reward. The best-rated tools are narrow, fast and light on permissions: GoFullPage, Text Blaze, Tactiq and Sider all hold 4.9, and Todoist holds 4.8. The worst-rated tools are heavy or require an account: Loom 2.7, Notion Web Clipper 2.9, Google Keep 2.5 and LastPass 1.8 ([Loom](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/liecbddmkiiihnedobmlmillhodjkdmb); [Notion Clipper](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/knheggckgoiihginacbkhaalnibhilkk); [Keep](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/lpcaedmchfhocbbapmcbpinfpgnhiddi)). For ProdLi, this favours its current posture. Features triggered by a user action (`activeTab`, `scripting`, context menus, keyboard commands) add no install warning. Features that run on every page need `<all_urls>` and the "Read and change all your data on all websites" warning. If an update adds a permission that triggers a warning, Chrome disables the extension until the user accepts ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/permission-warnings)). For an extension whose listing sells trust, that cost rules out inline text expansion, grammar underlines, inline translation bubbles, dark mode and video-speed controls.

Store policy points the same way. The Chrome Web Store requires a single, narrow purpose, and it names "Toolbars that provide a broad array of functionality" as a violation ([CWS quality guidelines](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/quality-guidelines)). Its FAQ says multi-feature extensions get extra scrutiny, but it explicitly accepts a new tab page with an AI chatbot and open-tab search ([CWS FAQ](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/quality-guidelines-faq)). ProdLi's listing name is "AI Meeting Notes & Daily Planner". A feature that saves something into that workspace, such as a screenshot, a clip or a tab session, is defensible under that purpose. A feature that changes how other websites look or behave is not.

On the desktop, OS vendors keep absorbing the most-installed utility categories. macOS Tahoe added clipboard history to Spotlight (kept for about 8 hours by default, up to 7 days), along with app actions and "Quick Keys" such as typing "ar" to add a reminder ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/do-more-with-spotlight-in-macos-tahoe/)). Windows' Snipping Tool gained OCR text extraction and screen recording ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/windows-snipping-tool-now-lets-you-create-animated-gif-recordings/)), and PowerToys took over the launcher. Raycast raised $30M to reach Windows and only reached a free public beta in November 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/25/raycast-raises-30m-to-bring-its-mac-productivity-app-to-windows-and-ios); [Impress Watch](https://forest.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/2073823.html)). A solo developer can't win the launcher, clipboard or window-management categories on features. ProdLi can win small parts of them only where the output feeds its own data: a clip that becomes a task, a screenshot whose text Ask can search, a Pomodoro visible in the menu bar.

Money tells the same story from the other side. The two desktop categories that drew unicorn rounds are both capture features ProdLi already has. **Wispr Flow raised $280M at a $2B valuation for system-wide dictation, and Granola raised $125M at $1.5B for botless meeting notes** ([SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/wispr-280-million-series-b-2-billion-valuation/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/granola-raises-125m-hits-1-5b-valuation-as-it-expands-from-meeting-notetaker-to-enterprise-ai-app/)). Automatic time tracking is a smaller paid niche: Rize charges $9.99–39.99 a month with no free tier ([Rize](https://rize.io/blog/time-tracking-software-pricing-comparison)). The most-installed categories (ad blocking, PDF, translation) are commodities. The categories with funding are capture and AI over personal data, where ProdLi already competes.

Two cautions follow. First, the near-term plan should be re-baselined. Of the five Tier 1 items, the trust pack (#1) and conversion mechanics (#5) are what remain. #2, #3 and #4 need only polish and a real-use check, not new builds. Second, shortcut slots are used up. Voice dictation into any text field shipped on `main` (Alt+Shift+D, `src/dictation/`), so the manifest now declares four commands: record, dictate, mark moment and quick add. Chrome caps suggested shortcuts at four per extension, and global ones (which work when Chrome isn't focused) must use Ctrl+Shift+0–9 ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/commands)). A screenshot shortcut would therefore need to replace one of these, rely on the user assigning it in chrome://extensions/shortcuts, or live in the palette (#4) instead. *(Corrected after writing: the first draft checked an older branch that lacked dictation.)*

Three judgements in this table are not obvious, so here is the reasoning. **Screenshot to note** ranks above read-aloud and translation because it is the largest consumer-chosen productivity job in the browser (GoFullPage's 11M). It also needs no new permission, and its output (an image plus OCR text that Ask can search) adds to ProdLi's archive. Translation and grammar fail that test: the user wants the result in the page they are on, so the value stays with Google Translate and Grammarly. **Clipboard history** ranks lower than its 110K Maccy installs suggest. Tahoe now gives every Mac user a basic version for free, and a ProdLi version only earns its place if clips sync to the web app and turn into tasks. Copied passwords also become a liability the moment history syncs. **Screen recording with share links** looks like a natural extension of ProdLi's call recorder. Its real cost is hosting and bandwidth, and Loom's 2.7-star rating shows how unforgiving users are when that goes wrong.

## Fifteen additions for the plan, and the list to refuse

These findings reinforce four parts of the existing plan. The meetings-first positioning holds up, because Tactiq's 4.9 stars at 1M users is the best quality-to-size signal in the browser, and Granola's valuation shows where the money is. Keeping Ask scoped to the user's own data is now essential: Gemini sits in Chrome's sidebar for free and Claude has 24M extension users, so a generic "chat with this page" panel has no room left. The MCP server (Tier 2 #10) matters more than it first appeared, because ProdLi's likeliest early adopters install Claude, Codex and Claude Code ahead of almost anything else. India pricing (Tier 1 #5) is reinforced too: the local anchor is ₹399 for ChatGPT Go, which was free for a year, so a ₹199 plan with UPI looks necessary, not optional.

The findings change three things. First, **system-wide dictation should move from Tier 3 to the first desktop item** once the app is signed. It is the desktop feature with the clearest willingness-to-pay evidence, and ProdLi's version can feed Speak tasks and notes directly. Granola gives dictation away free, so it should be sold as part of Pro, not as an add-on. Second, **Tier 3 #16 (mobile quick capture) should be split.** Widgets with habit streaks and share-sheet capture come first, because they are the only phone features with retention evidence or a direct match to existing desktop capture. Third, the plan should adopt a standing permission rule. **No feature may add a required host permission.** Anything that needs `<all_urls>` ships behind an optional permission requested at first use, or doesn't ship.

Two practical notes on the phone items. Widgets, share extensions, scanners and Live Activities all need native targets and development builds. The current setup verifies the Expo app through typecheck, tests and the web preview, not on devices, so items 5, 6, 11 and 15 carry more risk than their effort suggests until EAS device builds are routine. Separately, listing ProdLi on Edge Add-ons costs about a day with the same package. It's worth doing for the Edge store's audience, though Edge users can already install from the Chrome Web Store.

The refusal list matters as much as the build list. The plan's don't-build table should gain the following, each with a one-line reason. Ad blocking, coupons and password managers are off-purpose, saturated, and carry heavy permission or security liabilities; the Honey scandal also tightened affiliate rules from June 2025 ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/cws-policy-update-affiliate-ads-2025)). Dark mode, video speed, volume boost, grammar underlines and inline translation bubbles all need `<all_urls>` content scripts on every page and invite a "broad toolbar" rejection. PDF editing, an office suite, an authenticator and a standalone QR scanner are owned by Adobe, Microsoft, Google and the OS camera. A full launcher with app and file search, window management, a system monitor, a menu-bar organiser and keyboard remapping are free in the OS or as open source, and ProdLi's palette should stay scoped to ProdLi. System-wide text expansion depends on fragile keyboard hooks and Input Monitoring permission, and the browser picker covers the real need. A tab suspender is largely covered by Chrome's built-in memory saving (not verified this session), and the category is tiny. A bookmarks manager adds a new permission warning for a 400K-user niche. Hosted screen-recording links bring bandwidth bills and Loom's support burden. A multi-model chat picker turns ProdLi into Sider; MCP covers "works with my Claude" better. Pet or companion gamification in the style of Finch takes art and economy design, and it clashes with a professional brand. A watch app has no toolchain in the current setup and little demand evidence. Microdrama and short-video learning are India's growth categories, but they are not ProdLi's. Finally, a Firefox port should wait: Firefox's top 30 is entirely privacy, ad-blocking, video and translation tools, and ProdLi depends on Chrome-specific APIs such as offscreen documents and tab capture, whose Firefox support was not verified this session.

These conclusions rest on uneven evidence. Install counts are proxies: Chrome rounds to buckets, Homebrew counts reinstalls from a developer-heavy audience, mobile trackers disagree by hundreds of millions, and Windows has no public ranking at all. No source isolates the retention effect of share sheets, scanning or Live Activities, so their ranks rest on judgement plus Duolingo's widget evidence. The effort figures are estimates, not measurements against ProdLi's codebase.

## Conclusion

The install charts suggest a trap. The biggest numbers belong to utilities (ad blockers, PDF tools, translators, launchers), and copying them would grow ProdLi's feature list while shrinking its trust, its store standing and its focus. The useful lesson is narrower: **people reward tools that do one capture job instantly with few permissions, and the platforms keep giving away the rest.** ProdLi's advantage is the place where captured things end up. A screenshot, a tab set, a spoken ramble, a scanned receipt or a meeting all become tasks and notes that Ask can find later. Every addition above earns its rank by adding to that archive. Every refusal is something a platform already gives away free, or something that would touch every web page the user visits.

The code check matters most for the next month. ProdLi has already built much of what its own plan calls future work, so the bottleneck is no longer features. It is the trust pack, conversion mechanics, store approval, signed desktop builds and a working phone release. The cheapest high-value moves from this research (the tray timer with keep-awake, streak freezes, path-level blocks and read-aloud) together take about two weeks. After that, the plan's centre of gravity should move to the phone, which in India is where the users are, and to desktop dictation, which has the clearest evidence that people will pay.
